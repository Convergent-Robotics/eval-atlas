Mapping physical AI safety evaluations

How well does the existing evaluation landscape cover the risks of advanced physical AI? The atlas maps the published safety evaluations for robots and embodied AI systems and analyses the most pressing gaps in the literature.

Safe behaviour requires both a correct judgement of the situation and the operator's intent, and enough dexterity to carry it out robustly. Dexterity is required for some classes of safe behaviour (e.g. handling hazardous substances) but is orthogonal to others, such as refusing harmful requests. The current generation of physical AI safety evaluations covers either tasks that demand high dexterity or tasks that demand cognitive abilities such as causal or strategic reasoning, but rarely both.

Several gaps stand out in the current landscape of physical AI safety evaluations.

Inclusion criteria

The included works measure safety-relevant behaviour or capability of a robot or embodied AI system. Only works that propose a concrete protocol that can be applied by third parties are included. Purely theoretical work is out of scope. Mitigations and runtime monitors are not included on their own merit. Autonomous driving, robustness-only studies and capability benchmarks without a safety reading are left out, as are evaluations with no link to robotics. An evaluation study is a one-off measurement with a reusable protocol but no fixed task set.

Scoring methodology

Each entry is tagged by application domain, system architecture and threat model, and scored for task complexity, dexterity, cognition and relevance to catastrophic risk. Scores are judgements made on the basis of the paper and its project page. Each score carries a one-line reason in the entry's detail panel. Where an entry spans several levels, it is scored by its most demanding tasks.

Bottleneck

Which demand currently limits results on the evaluation: cognition (models fail to notice or reason about the hazard), dexterity (models fail to act on it safely), or both.

Harm classes

Local physical harm covers everyday accidents and injuries. The other classes cover violence and weapons, biological uplift, capture of infrastructure or robot fleets, loss of control (self-modification, replication, resisting shutdown), concentration of power and gradual disempowerment, plus privacy and surveillance, and discrimination and stereotyping.

Sources

Three 2026 surveys (Safety in Embodied AI; Vision-Language-Action Safety; Security of Foundation-Model-Powered Embodied Agents), targeted searches for healthcare, humanoids, multi-robot systems, world models, drones, cyber and loss-of-control evaluations, and regular searches of new arXiv submissions. First compiled September 2026.

Corrections and additions

If an evaluation is missing or mis-scored, please open an issue or pull request in the project repository. Each entry is a single JSON file in db/entries/.
